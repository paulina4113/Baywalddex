Pokédex style education 
Software that visitors (kids) can use to get information on plants, animals, insects etc. inside the park by scanning QR-codes/barcodes spread across the park. At the end of their stay they can go to the reception and get a summary of all the things they have scanned and get a diploma or a price based on how many things they have scanned.

Business case 
There are a few ways we can integrate the Pokédex software for visitor-use. The purpose of the software is firstly, giving kids a fun and educational experience and secondly, having a way of tracking people inside the park. This can contribute to logistical improvements in the park and making sure unrestricted areas are not accessed by unauthorized persons. 

Hand-held scanners 
Pros 
• Creates a strong, “Pokédexlike” experience that feels special and engaging 
• Very suitable for children; no personal smartphone needed 
• Fewer distractions compared to phones (no messages, games, social media) 
• Robust and reliable for outdoor use 
• Full control over hardware, software, and content by the organization 
• Consistent tracking and data collection across all users 
• Can work offline and synchronize data later 

Cons 
• Higher initial costs due to hardware purchase 
• Ongoing maintenance (charging, cleaning, repairs) 
• Risk of loss or damage 
• Limited scalability: more visitors require more devices 
• Logistics required for issuing and collecting devices 
• Software updates can be slower to roll out 

Installable application 
Pros 
• Much lower hardware costs (visitors use their own devices) 
• Easy to scale for large visitor numbers 
• Faster and cheaper to develop and update 
• No physical device management required 
• Immediate updates via app stores or web apps 
• Can integrate easily with modern features like GPS and notifications 

Cons 
• Less immersive and “special” than a dedicated device 
• Not all children have or are allowed to use smartphones 
• Battery, connectivity, and device compatibility issues 
• More distractions from other apps 
• Tracking accuracy is less consistent due to privacy settings 
• Requires users to install or access an app, which some may resist 

General pros and cons of giving kids an educational experience 
Pros 
• Encourages active learning through exploration 
• Increases knowledge retention compared to passive learning 
• Makes education fun and motivating through gamification 
• Supports curiosity and independent discovery 
• Builds longterm awareness and respect for nature 
• Aligns with the park’s primary goal of protecting the environment 
• By giving children a positive and educational experience, both they and the people around them learn more about the importance of nature and the necessity of protecting it 
• Creates future visitors who are more likely to behave responsibly in natural areas 

Cons 
• Development of highquality educational content requires time and expertise 
• Some children may focus more on rewards than learning goals 
• Risk of overstimulation if the experience becomes too gamefocused 
• Requires a careful balance between entertainment and accurate information 
• Needs inclusive design to support different ages and learning styles 

 

Enhancing Educational Interaction in the Park 
Digital technologies, when designed specifically for youth, can help combat the "extinction of experience"—the trend of children spending less time in green spaces—by turning the outdoors into an engaging learning environment. 

1. Augmented Reality (AR) at Empty Enclosures 
A common challenge in the park is that animals (like wolves, bison, or lynx) are often hiding or sleeping, causing children to lose patience quickly. By placing QR codes at the viewing platforms, visitors can scan them to unlock an Augmented Reality (AR) experience or high-quality video footage on their screens, allowing them to learn about the animal's behaviour even if the physical animal is out of sight. 

 2. Interactive Digital Scavenger Hunts 
Instead of just walking a trail, the app or scanner can offer a gamified scavenger hunt tailored for families. Children can be prompted to find and scan QR codes hidden near specific ancient trees, rock formations, or animal enclosures to collect "digital badges". This keeps younger visitors actively observing their surroundings rather than getting bored on long hikes. 

3. Digital Field Guides 
The digital interface can serve as an interactive field guide. When a family scans a QR code near a specific plant or habitat, the screen can display interactive quizzes, audio recordings of local bird calls, or historical tales about the park's "let nature be nature" philosophy, reducing the need for the park to build and maintain thousands of expensive, physical information boards.
